When the answer keeps changing

You may have tried a lot already.

Massage, stretching, PT exercises, chiropractic, mobility work, strengthening—any of these can be useful. The difficult part is that different approaches often come with different, confident explanations for the same experience.

The problem is not that every explanation is wrong. It is that an explanation only becomes useful when it helps us make and repeat a meaningful change.

  • “This is tight.”
  • “This is weak.”
  • “Your posture is wrong.”
  • “Your fascia is restricted.”

Pain and tension are real. Our explanation for them still has to be tested.

The Process

Notice. Test. Adapt. Integrate.

This is not a straight line or a fixed protocol. It is a repeating loop that helps us learn what changes, build more options, and see what holds up outside the session.

Learn
adjust
repeat
01 · Notice

What is actually happening?

We start with what hurts, feels restricted, or matters to you. We look at timing, position, load, breathing, tension, movement, and side-to-side differences. Sensation is useful information, but it is not automatically the explanation.

→
02 · Test

What changes it?

Hands-on bodywork, movement, pressure, traction, joint movement, breathing, relaxation, or self-release become experiments. The key question is simple: what changed? If something repeatedly does nothing useful, we change the experiment.

→
03 · Adapt

Can we create more options?

We move beyond temporary table relief by exploring different positions, loads, directions, tasks, movement problems, and sometimes play. The aim is not one “correct” pattern. It is greater adaptability.

→
04 · Integrate

Does it hold up outside the session?

Can you notice tension earlier, change strategy yourself, tolerate a different load, and carry what we learned into walking, lifting, training, work, sport, or parenting? Then the loop starts again.

→

Why Four Sessions

One session can tell us something. Four sessions let us learn.

Four sessions are not a promise that every problem will be solved. They are the minimum useful period for an iterative process: enough time to test, revisit, and refine what helps.

Immediate response → durable change

See whether a shift lasts, returns, or changes under different conditions.

Coincidence → repeatable effect

Retest what helped instead of assuming the first result tells the whole story.

Table relief → movement transfer

Find out whether a change carries into the tasks and activities that matter.

Practitioner-created → client-created

Build ways for you to influence how you feel without always needing treatment.

FAQ

Common questions.

Is this massage?

Yes, but not a fixed routine. Sessions may also include movement, breath, sustained pressure, traction, guided attention, mobility work, and reassessment.

Is this physical therapy?

No. I do not diagnose or provide physical therapy. This is therapeutic bodywork and movement education.

Do I need four sessions?

No. Single sessions remain available. Four sessions are recommended because they give us time to distinguish an immediate response from durable change, see what repeats, and apply what we learn to movement.

Do I need to know whether I need myofascial release, sports massage, or another technique?

No. Tell me what you feel, what you want to do, and what you have already tried. We choose tools based on how you respond rather than asking you to select a modality first.

Do you work with injuries or surgery history?

Yes, when bodywork is appropriate and you have appropriate medical clearance. Include relevant history on your intake form.
