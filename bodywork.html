Who It Is For

For people who want hands-on work and a way forward.

You may be a good fit if…

  • Massage helps for a while, but the same tension or restriction keeps returning.
  • You feel stiff or limited and want to move more comfortably.
  • You want to return to training, sport, or a more active life.
  • You want hands-on work plus practical things you can do yourself.
  • You prefer an individualized approach over a fixed massage routine.

This may not be the right fit if…

  • You want a predictable spa-massage routine every visit.
  • You want someone to fix the problem without your participation or feedback.
  • You equate effective work only with very firm, fast, or painful pressure.
Approach

Bodywork and movement do not have to be separate.

Recurring tension and movement limitations can involve more than one thing: how you brace, breathe, move, recover, train, respond to stress, and adapt to old injuries or habits.

I use hands-on work, movement, and reassessment to notice what responds. The goal is not to force your body into a perfect position; it is to help you find useful changes and learn what gives you more options.

Depending on what is useful, sessions may draw from therapeutic massage, myofascial release, sports-massage techniques, deep-tissue and trigger-point or neuromuscular approaches, assisted stretching and mobility, movement assessment, self-myofascial release, breath and relaxation practices, and fascia-focused bodywork.

FAQ

Common questions.

Is this massage?

Yes, but not a fixed routine. Sessions may also include movement, breath, sustained holds, guided attention, mobility work, and reassessment.

Is this physical therapy?

No. I do not diagnose or provide physical therapy. This is therapeutic bodywork and movement education.

Do I need four sessions?

No. Single sessions remain available. The four-session process is the recommended starting point because it gives us enough continuity to see what changes, what returns, work hands-on more than once, and apply what we learn to movement.

Do I need to know whether I need myofascial release, sports massage, or another technique?

No. You can tell me what you feel, what you want to do, and what you have already tried. We choose tools based on how you respond rather than requiring you to select a modality first.

Do you work with injuries or surgery history?

Yes, when bodywork is appropriate and you have appropriate medical clearance. Include relevant history on your intake form.
