04 / How the programming works

Practice, observe,
adjust.

The written program and demonstrations begin an ongoing exchange. What happens in practice helps shape the next cycle.

  1. Understand the person

    We identify current capacities, limitations, interests, and goals.

  2. Build a training cycle

    We select practices and starting prescriptions around those needs, with written instructions and video demonstrations.

  3. Practice and record

    You work independently and film selected material, including technical tasks and anything that needs clarification.

  4. Review together

    We review footage and provide feedback and technical guidance. Your observations and questions are part of the exchange.

  5. Change the program

    We modify workload, develop progressions, and introduce material as capacity and skill change.

05 / Principles behind the practice

A structure with
room to explore.

Preparation feeds the skill

Strength and physical preparation support the parkour and acrobatics. The practices are intended to connect into a cohesive whole.

Quality sets the next step

The quality and consistency of a practice help determine when to change its range, difficulty, or complexity. Earlier variations remain available as capacity develops.

Connect the whole body

Arm swings, stepping tasks, jumps, and inversions repeatedly ask how force and timing connect the limbs through the torso.

Use the tools creatively

Creative work is an invitation to make something with the available skills and surroundings. Some tasks work best as small workshops of exploration rather than timed sets.

Let practice inform the plan

Notice what resonates, record what needs attention, and adjust the workload. The program invites student feedback as the phases develop.

Attend to the experience

Awareness and enjoyment have a place alongside physical capacity and technical skill.
